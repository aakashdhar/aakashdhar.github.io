# Portfolio copy review — for EU technical-PM / EIBS hiring

**Bottom line first:** this site is already well above the portfolio average. The writing is mostly specific, first-person, and understated — exactly what German, Dutch, Nordic, and French tech recruiters respond to. Numbers are hedged ("about 20%", "roughly a quarter") instead of inflated, which reads as honest rather than salesy. So most of what follows is targeted, not wholesale. The real work is in three places: cutting a repeated phrase, trimming the keyword-farm tag chips, and resolving one positioning tension between "AI-forward" and "enterprise integration."

## Overall tone notes

1. **The one habit to fix site-wide is repetition, not jargon.** "In production," "AI systems," and "mid-build takeover" each recur enough to shift from fact to slogan. Your jargon problem is small; your echo problem is the thing that occasionally makes strong copy sound automated. Say each strong claim once, in the place it lands hardest.

2. **The prose is a full register better than the tag chips.** Everywhere the writing is in sentences, it's specific and human; everywhere it collapses into chips, it drifts toward the keyword farm ("Evidence-based PM," "AI-native Workflow," "Systems Design," "Product Builder"). Rule of thumb: a chip should name a thing a technical interviewer could ask a follow-up question about — an API, a platform, a standard, a constraint — not restate the pitch as an adjective.

3. **There's a real positioning tension between "AI-forward" and EIBS.** The site leans hard on agentic AI, RAG pipelines, and multi-agent systems. That's genuine and current, but for a conservative enterprise-integration or business-systems recruiter in Germany or the Netherlands, front-loaded AI language can read as hype and quietly undercut the CRM/ERP/API integration credibility that's actually your deepest evidence. You don't need to restructure — but consider letting the enterprise-integration track (World Bank, the three-system Link Broadband integration, the CRM/ERP history) carry equal visual and verbal weight to the AI tooling, so the reader sees a systems-integration PM who also builds AI, not an AI enthusiast who used to integrate systems.

4. **Your best credibility asset is the hedged number — protect it.** "About 20%," "roughly a quarter," "around 30% over nine months" are why the metrics read as measured rather than invented. Never let a later edit round these into confident whole figures. If anything, the same honesty could extend to a couple of the AI-tooling claims, which are currently stated more absolutely than the engineering ones.

5. **Where the voice already comes through, leave it fully alone.** "The space between what the client said and what the engineer heard," "I need ten minutes with the ticket and the person who wrote it," "managing people is harder than managing code," "something questionable for dinner" — these are the lines a recruiter remembers. The risk in a polish pass is sanding these down toward a neutral "professional" tone. The goal is the opposite: cut the slogans and the chips *so that these lines stand out more*, not less.
