## Overall tone notes

1. **The writing is already good — resist the urge to "improve" the strong sections.** The biggest risk to this site is over-editing. The hedged numbers, the dry personal lines, and the "buck stops with me" honesty are assets. Most of my suggestions above are *cuts and reframes*, not rewrites, because there is very little jargon left to strip.

2. **Your EIBS / enterprise-integration specialism is under-surfaced relative to how central it is to your search.** The AI-tooling story dominates the top of the site; the integration evidence (CRM, ERP, SP-API, HubSpot, ERP rebuilds) is real and strong but framed as either "past developer work" or buried in tag stacks. Pull the integration thread forward in the hero and reframe section 06 so a recruiter searching for EIBS delivery sees it in the first screen, not the sixth.

3. **Tag/pill stacks are the last keyword-farm residue.** Everywhere else you write in sentences; the tags revert to LinkedIn-skill-cloud mode. Cap them at three per card and keep only proper nouns (Title 22, HubSpot, GPT-4o, SP-API) over category words (Compliance, Multi-party, Automation). Specific nouns read as evidence; categories read as SEO.

4. **You have three or four "writerly" flourishes — that's about one too many, not a systemic problem.** "Someone has to live in that gap," "the most honest brief you can have," "the tools are the same." Each one on its own is fine; together they establish a pattern a skeptical reader starts to notice. Trim the About one (the most exposed) and you can keep the rest.

5. **Consistency of hedging is a strength — protect it.** You correctly write "about 30%", "roughly 25–30%", "around a quarter." Do not let any future edit convert these to clean round numbers. In EU hiring culture the hedge is the credibility signal; a page full of exact percentages reads as marketing, a page of honest estimates reads as someone who actually measured.
