/ About

Who I am.

I wrote code for eight and a half years before switching sides.

I was a developer first. That changes how I manage.

I started as a developer because I wanted to know how things work. After eight and a half years of backends, APIs, and system architecture, you stop seeing software as code and start seeing it as a series of decisions, trade-offs, and consequences that someone has to own.

What pulled me toward project management wasn't the frameworks. It was noticing that most of the risk on a software project sits in the gap between what the client asked for and what the engineer built. I'd spent years on the engineering side of that gap and wanted to work both.

Now at BetaCraft I build the internal AI tooling our delivery runs on, alongside managing four client engagements that span enterprise integration work and AI platforms.

[01]I read the codebase before I scope the work

No PM-shaped proxy between you and engineering. I can challenge an estimate, spot an architecture risk, and still write the client update, without a translator.

[02]I build the tooling, not just the process

I built, documented, and shipped the vibe-* dev framework and the PM skills system myself. Both are in daily use.

[03]The buck stops with me

Client delivery at BetaCraft runs from brief to sign-off under one person. Scope changes, blockers, and budget conversations come to me directly. There is no escalation layer above me, so when something slips, it is mine to own and mine to fix.
