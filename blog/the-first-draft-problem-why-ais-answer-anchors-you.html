The First Draft Problem: Why AI's Answer Anchors You

01

A plan I did not quite write

I asked a tool to draft a delivery plan for a new piece of work, to save myself the blank page. It gave me something sensible in seconds. I edited it, tightened it, shipped it. Only later did I notice that the whole plan was still shaped like the one the model handed me. I had improved its answer. I had not really written my own.

That is the quiet cost nobody warns you about. It is not that the AI's first draft is wrong. It is that, right or wrong, it becomes the thing you react to instead of the thing you would have thought of yourself.

It took me a while to even notice, because the plan was good. Nobody would have looked at it and seen a problem. That is what made it worth writing about. The failure was invisible from the outside, and only I knew that the shape of it was not really mine, that I had graded the model's homework instead of doing my own.

02

Why the anchor holds

There is an old, well-studied pull in how people think: the first number, the first framing, the first draft sets the ground, and everything after adjusts from it rather than starting fresh. A blank page is uncomfortable precisely because it makes you generate the frame. AI removes that discomfort, which feels like a gift, and the hidden price is that you inherit its frame without ever choosing it.

Editing feels like thinking. It is not the same thing. When I edit, I stay inside the shape I was given and make it better. When I start cold, I might arrive somewhere the model would never have gone, because I am carrying context it does not have. The best version of the work is sometimes on a road the first draft quietly closed off.

The insidious part is that a good first draft anchors harder than a bad one. A bad draft you throw out and start fresh. A good one you keep, and improve, and defend, and by the end you are so invested in making it better that it never occurs to you it was the wrong starting point. Competence is a more comfortable cage than incompetence.

A first draft doesn't just save you the blank page. It decides which page you're on.

03

What I do about it

Not stop using AI for first drafts, that would be daft, it genuinely saves time on the things that have one right shape. But for anything where the framing is the actual value, the plan, the approach, the way we cut the problem up, I try to write my own rough version first, before I look at the model's. Ninety seconds of my own bad draft is enough to anchor me to my own thinking. Then the tool becomes a second opinion instead of the first word.

It is a small discipline and an annoying one, because the whole appeal of the tool is skipping exactly that ninety seconds. But those ninety seconds are where my own read of the problem gets onto the page, and once the model's version is in front of me, that read is much harder to reach.

I do not always manage it. Some days the pull of the shortcut wins and I let the model frame the thing, and mostly it is fine, because plenty of work does have one sensible shape and my own version would have landed in the same place. The discipline is knowing which work is which: where the framing is the whole value, and where it genuinely does not matter who drew the first line.

04

The tell that you skipped it

The tell that I have skipped this is a subtle one. The work is fine, defensible, nobody would object. It just does not have anything in it that is particularly mine. That is what an unexamined first draft produces: competent, reasonable output shaped by a mind that was not actually in the room, which is a fine description of most forgettable work.

So the small ritual stays. Bad draft first, then the tool. It costs me ninety seconds and a little bit of ego, because my rough version is always worse than the model's polished one. But mine is mine, built from context the model never had, and once in a while it goes somewhere genuinely better than the confident first answer would ever have let me reach. That is worth ninety seconds and the bruise.

So next time you reach for a tool to skip the blank page, worth asking: are you using its draft to sharpen your thinking, or quietly adopting its thinking as your own?
